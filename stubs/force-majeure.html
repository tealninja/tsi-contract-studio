1. Definition. Force Majeure means an event or circumstance beyond the reasonable control of the affected party that it could not have avoided or overcome by the exercise of reasonable diligence, including acts of God, war, terrorism, civil unrest, epidemic, fire, flood, earthquake, severe weather, embargo, strike or labor dispute not specific to the affected party's workforce, and act of a governmental authority. Force Majeure does not include a party's inability to pay, changes in market or financial conditions, or the failure of a subcontractor except where that failure is itself caused by an event of Force Majeure.
  2. Relief. Neither Seller nor Buyer is in breach of this Agreement to the extent its performance, other than an obligation to pay money then due, is prevented or delayed by Force Majeure. The affected party's obligations are suspended for the duration of the event and to the extent of the prevention or delay.
  3. Notice. The affected party shall give written notice to the other party within seven days of becoming aware of the event, describing the event, the obligations affected, and the anticipated duration, and shall give a further notice when the event ends. [DRAFT NOTE: Confirm the 7-day notice window against the project's reporting cadence; some owners require 48 hours for site-affecting events.]
  4. Mitigation. The affected party shall use reasonable efforts to mitigate the effect of the event, to continue performance of unaffected obligations, and to resume affected performance as soon as reasonably practicable. It shall keep the other party informed of the steps taken.
  5. Effect on schedule and liquidated damages. Where Force Majeure delays a milestone, the affected dates, including the dates for Commissioning and Substantial Completion, are extended by the period of delay, and no liquidated damages (LD) accrue for delay attributable to the event. An extension of time is the affected party's sole remedy for Force Majeure; neither party owes the other compensation for costs caused by the event. [INT: This is a time-only relief clause — no cost recovery either way. Flag if the commercial team expects Seller standby costs to be reimbursable.]
  6. Prolonged Force Majeure. If an event of Force Majeure continues for more than 120 consecutive days, or for more than 180 days in the aggregate, either party may terminate this Agreement on written notice. On termination under this clause, Buyer shall pay Seller for Equipment delivered and work performed up to the date of termination, together with the reasonable, documented cost of demobilization, without any further liability of either party.
