1. Definition. Confidential Information means non-public information disclosed by one party (the disclosing party) to the other (the receiving party) under this Agreement, whether disclosed in writing, orally, or by inspection, that is marked confidential or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential from its nature or the circumstances of disclosure. It includes designs, drawings, pricing, and the technical documentation for the Equipment.
  2. Obligations. The receiving party shall use Confidential Information only to perform or exercise its rights under this Agreement, shall protect it with at least the degree of care it uses for its own confidential information of like importance and no less than reasonable care, and shall disclose it only to its employees, affiliates, and advisors who need it for that purpose and are bound by confidentiality obligations no less protective than these.
  3. Exclusions. Confidential Information does not include information that (a) is or becomes public other than through the receiving party's breach, (b) was rightfully known to the receiving party without obligation of confidence before disclosure, (c) is rightfully received from a third party without obligation of confidence, or (d) is independently developed by the receiving party without use of the Confidential Information.
  4. Compelled disclosure. The receiving party may disclose Confidential Information to the extent required by law or court or regulatory order, provided that, where lawful, it gives the disclosing party prompt notice and reasonable cooperation to seek protective treatment, and discloses only the portion required.   5. Term and survival. The obligations in this Article continue for five years after termination or expiration of the Agreement, except that Confidential Information that is a trade secret remains protected for as long as it qualifies as a trade secret under applicable law. [INT: Five-year tail is the house default; align with the confidentiality carve-out in the limitation-of-liability stub, which lifts the cap for breach of this Article.]
  6. Return or destruction. On the disclosing party's written request or on termination of the Agreement, the receiving party shall return or destroy the Confidential Information in its possession and, on request, certify the destruction. The receiving party may retain one archival copy solely to evidence compliance and copies held in routine backup, in each case subject to the continuing obligations of this Article.
  7. Injunctive relief. The parties agree that a breach of this Article may cause harm for which monetary damages are inadequate, and that the disclosing party is entitled to seek injunctive relief in addition to any other remedy, without the need to post a bond where permitted by law.   8. No license. Disclosure of Confidential Information does not grant the receiving party any license or ownership in the disclosing party's Intellectual Property, which is governed by the Article on IP ownership and license.
