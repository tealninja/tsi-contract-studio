This annex sets the mechanical design, materials, and workmanship standards for the Equipment supplied by Seller — dryer sections, rolls, frames, and rotating assemblies. Where a value is shown for confirmation it is project-specific and shall be verified against the process datasheet before release for manufacture.

1. Materials of construction

Structural steelASTM A36 / A572 Gr. 50, painted per the coatings annex
Machined framescast iron ASTM A48 Class 40 or fabricated and stress-relieved steel
Rolls (shells)centrifugally cast iron or fabricated steel, dynamically balanced
Wetted / washdown partsstainless steel AISI 304L, or 316L in chemically aggressive zones
ShaftingASTM A322 alloy steel, quench-and-tempered
FastenersSAE Gr. 5 / Gr. 8 or ISO 8.8 / 10.9; stainless A2-70 in wet-end

[DRAFT NOTE: Confirm stainless grade for wet-end per the process chemistry (chloride exposure drives 304L vs 316L).]

2. Welding and pressure parts

Welding of load-bearing and pressure-retaining components is performed to ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code, Section IX, by qualified procedures and welders. Steam-heated dryer cylinders and any pressure vessel within the supply are designed, fabricated, and stamped to ASME BPVC Section VIII, Division 1. Structural welds follow AWS D1.1. Weld inspection extent:

Structural welds100% visual; magnetic-particle on primary load paths
Pressure weldsradiographic or ultrasonic per ASME VIII acceptance
Post-weld heat treatmentas required by code and wall thickness

[INT: Dryer cylinder design pressure and MAWP drive the code stamp requirement; confirm on the mechanical datasheet.]

3. Fasteners and torque

Bolted joints are torqued to the values on the assembly drawings, developed from the fastener grade, thread, and lubricated condition. Critical joints (bearing housings, coupling hubs, roll journals) are recorded on a torque log at assembly and re-checked during installation.

Torque methodcalibrated wrench; hydraulic tensioning above M36
Thread conditionlightly oiled unless drawing specifies dry or anti-seize
Retentionmechanical (locking plates, safety wire); thread-locker only where specified

4. Alignment and balance tolerances

Coupling alignment≤ 0.05 mm parallel offset, ≤ 0.05 mm/100 mm angular, cold
Roll parallelism≤ 0.10 mm over the working face
Rotating balanceISO 21940-11 grade G 2.5 for rolls and drive rotors
Residual runout≤ 25 µm TIR at journals
Vibration acceptanceISO 10816 zone A/B at rated speed

[DRAFT NOTE: Tighten roll parallelism if the grade/basis-weight target requires; confirm with the paper-machine specialist.]

5. Lubrication

Bearings are grease- or oil-lubricated per the component supplier's schedule. A consolidated lubrication chart listing point, lubricant type, quantity, and interval is furnished with the Equipment. Circulating-oil systems, where supplied, include filtration to ISO 4406 cleanliness class   and cooling sized for the Site ambient.

6. Guarding and safety

All rotating and nip points reachable in normal operation are guarded to ANSI B11 and ISO 14120, with interlocked access where guards must open for threading or maintenance. Guards are removable without cutting and are painted per the coatings annex.

7. Piping

Process and utility piping within the supply is designed to ASME B31.1 (power/steam) or B31.3 (process) as applicable, with materials, wall thickness, and supports per the piping datasheet. Steam and condensate lines are insulated; hydraulic and lubrication lines are flushed to the specified cleanliness before commissioning.

8. Nameplates

Each major assembly carries a stainless nameplate with manufacturer, model, serial number, mass, rated speed, and applicable code stamp. Nameplates are mechanically fastened and legible after painting. [ATTY: Confirm serial-number and code-stamp traceability requirements match the warranty and any lender/insurer conditions.]
