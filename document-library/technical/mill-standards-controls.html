2. I/O standards

  • Analog inputs: 4–20 mA, 2-wire where possible, individually fused, with signal conditioning and channel-level diagnostics.
  • RTD / thermocouple inputs to the sensor standard on the instrument datasheet; RTDs are 3-wire Pt100.
  • Discrete I/O: 24 V DC, isolated, LED-indicated, with 20% installed spare capacity per rack minimum.
  • Safety I/O routed through a separate safety controller rated to the required SIL; not commingled with the process controller.

4. Batch and information models

Where the process is procedural, control is structured to ISA-88 (physical model, procedures, phases) for portability and clear state handling. Data exchange with the Buyer manufacturing systems follows the ISA-95 equipment and information hierarchy so that production, quality, and downtime data map to the mill MES without bespoke translation.

5. Alarms

Alarm design follows ISA-18.2: every alarm is rationalized, prioritized, and actionable, with defined setpoint, deadband, and operator response. Nuisance and standing alarms are eliminated before SAT. Alarm rate targets and shelving rules are agreed with the Buyer operations team.

6. Tag naming

Every instrument, loop, and I/O point carries a unique tag from the project tag register, consistent across the P&IDs, loop diagrams, PLC program, and HMI. Tag structure follows ISA-5.1 identification (measured variable + loop number + suffix), extended by the mill area/unit convention.

7. Cybersecurity baseline

The control system is delivered hardened to a baseline aligned with IEC 62443: default credentials removed, unused ports and services disabled, role-based access with named accounts, signed firmware where supported, and a documented backup of controller, HMI, and drive configurations at handover. No remote access is enabled without the Buyer's written authorization and an agreed secure method.
